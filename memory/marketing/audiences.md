# audiences.md — Segments

Six segments. Each row is a writable prompt: what they feel, what they object to, what they want, where they live. Author-publishing market only — PagePerfect is a book typesetting tool, not a dev tool or a generic SaaS.

## 1. KDP-first indie / self-published author

- **Profile**: writing or finishing a 60-90k word manuscript bound for Amazon KDP. ~70% of indie publishing volume. Sub-segments: fiction (genre — romance, thriller, fantasy, sci-fi) and non-fiction (business, memoir, self-help, technical). Has Vellum-envy (Mac-only, $500) or has bounced off Atticus ($147). Considers the manuscript "done" and wants to move on to launch.
- **Pain**: doesn't know what makes a book look "professional." Their Word doc, exported to PDF, looks amateur. Doesn't have the time or budget for a designer. Has had a file rejected by KDP at least once or fears it.
- **Objection**: "Will this look as good as a real book? Can I use it for KDP? Does it support my page size? Am I going to get rejected?"
- **Desired outcome**: paste manuscript, pick a template, get a print-ready PDF that doesn't embarrass them — free preview first, paid clean export when ready.
- **Register**: plain English. Define terms the first time (baseline grid, bleed, gutter, drop cap). Show, don't tell — the editor preview is the headline argument.
- **Channels**: Google ("format book for kdp"), r/selfpublish, r/PubTips, r/writing, KDP Community forums, KDP-focused Facebook groups (KDP Community, KDP Roadmap), Kindlepreneur newsletter, Self-Publishing Show podcast, NaNoWriMo (especially post-November).
- **Headline hook**: "Paste your manuscript. Pick a template. Get a print-ready book."

## 2. IngramSpark author (premium distribution, library / bookstore aspiration)

- **Profile**: hybrid distribution — typically KDP **and** IngramSpark. Higher craft sensibility. Cares about library and bookstore placement. Often a second-or-later book. May already use a designer for covers and want to bring interior typesetting in-house.
- **Pain**: IngramSpark's file requirements are strict (PDF/X-1a, embedded fonts, exact trim + bleed). Files get rejected for spine-width math errors, missing crop marks, or font subsetting issues. Designer round-trips are slow and expensive.
- **Objection**: "Does the output meet IngramSpark spec on first upload? Spine width? Bleed? Crop marks? PDF/X-1a? Can I update on a revision without paying again?"
- **Desired outcome**: a tool whose output IngramSpark accepts without rework. Reproducible across a backlist.
- **Register**: precise. Print specs are not negotiable. Cite IngramSpark's own help-page section + retrieval date when stating a hard number.
- **Channels**: IngramSpark blog and partner ecosystem, ALLi (Alliance of Independent Authors), Jane Friedman's newsletter, IBPA (Independent Book Publishers Association), The Creative Penn podcast.
- **Headline hook**: "IngramSpark-spec PDFs. PDF/X-1a, embedded fonts, exact trim. First upload accepted."

## 3. Lulu author (integration partner channel)

- **Profile**: smaller volume than KDP / IngramSpark, but PagePerfect's most directly integrated distribution channel — Lulu xPress API integration exists. Often authors who value the print-on-demand workflow without the Amazon ecosystem, or who want author-direct sales via Lulu's storefront.
- **Pain**: Lulu's interior file requirements differ from KDP's and IngramSpark's. Authors don't want to maintain three versions of the same file.
- **Objection**: "Can I send the order directly to Lulu? Are the templates Lulu-tested? How does pricing work for a single proof copy?"
- **Desired outcome**: compile once, fulfil via Lulu without rework. Studio-tier feature.
- **Register**: practical, workflow-led. Lulu is the channel users have a relationship with, so the language should respect that.
- **Channels**: Lulu's own author community, Lulu blog, ALLi (overlap), genre-specific indie communities.
- **Headline hook**: "Compile here, print at Lulu. One-click handoff."

## 4. Hybrid author with backlist (working indie, multiple books, opinions)

- **Profile**: 2-10 published books. Distributes via small press for some titles and self-typesets others. Uses InDesign sparingly or pays a designer. Knows the difference between Garamond and Caslon. Sells well enough to care about reorder economics. Often opinionated about hyphenation, drop caps, hanging footnotes, non-Latin scripts.
- **Pain**: per-book design costs erode margin. InDesign is a pain to update for typos. Vellum doesn't support what they want (poetry, footnotes, custom hierarchies, screenplays). Atticus is "fine for the basics" but doesn't reach their typographic ceiling.
- **Objection**: "Can I control hyphenation? Drop caps? Hanging footnotes? Subset fonts? What's the typography grade? Can I batch-update a backlist?"
- **Desired outcome**: a tool that respects them — explicit control, A-grade typography, KDP / IngramSpark / Lulu pipelines, batch updates across a backlist.
- **Register**: precise typographic language. Widows / orphans, baseline grid, optical alignment, ligatures, lining vs old-style figures — get the details right or lose them.
- **Channels**: Joanna Penn's audience, Self-Publishing Show, 20Booksto50K (Facebook group), Reedsy author-side, genre guilds (SFWA, MWA, RWA, HWA).
- **Headline hook**: "Production typography for working authors. 15 templates. 19 page sizes. Print-ready, every time."

## 5. Book coach / freelance editor (multiplier segment)

- **Profile**: serves multiple author clients per year. Found on Reedsy Marketplace, EFA (Editorial Freelancers Association), ACES, ALLi member directories, Authors Guild networks. Recommends a typesetting tool to the authors they coach or edit — one happy coach can route 10-50 authors a year.
- **Pain**: their authors hand them a finished manuscript and expect a print-ready file. The coach either pays a designer (margin loss) or hands the author off to a tool that produces inconsistent output (reputation risk).
- **Objection**: "Is the output good enough that I'd put my name on it? Can I learn it once and recommend it to every client? Is there a partner programme?"
- **Desired outcome**: a tool whose output reflects well on them. Predictable across genres. Optional bundle / referral arrangement.
- **Register**: peer-to-peer, evidence-led. Coaches are tool buyers who serve buyers — write to them as informed gatekeepers.
- **Channels**: Reedsy Marketplace, EFA, ACES, ALLi, Book Launchers, Self-Publishing School, ProBookEditors, EditMojo, LinkedIn editorial circles.
- **Headline hook**: "The typesetting tool you recommend once and stop worrying about."

## 6. Academic / professional author (specialised wedge)

- **Profile**: writing a dissertation, monograph, technical manual, conference proceedings, or research-heavy book. Uses LaTeX (or fights with Word). Cites in BibTeX. Cares about footnotes, bibliography style, double-spaced submission rules, citation accuracy.
- **Pain**: LaTeX learning curve is brutal. Word's footnote handling collapses past a certain length. University templates are outdated and ugly. Submission rules vary by institution.
- **Objection**: "Does it handle citations? Hanging footnotes? Equations? Tables? Specialist content (multilingual, math, code blocks)? Will my advisor / publisher accept the output?"
- **Desired outcome**: a manuscript-to-PDF pipeline that respects academic conventions — Chicago, Thesis, Symphony templates, BibTeX support, deterministic compile, reproducible build metadata.
- **Register**: precise, evidence-led. Citations are the wedge — get them right.
- **Channels**: r/AskAcademia, r/PhD, academic Twitter / Bluesky, university writing-centre forums, professional society newsletters.
- **Headline hook**: "Dissertation typography that doesn't fight you. BibTeX-native. Print-ready."

## Anti-segments (NOT our audience)

- Crypto / Web3 / DeFi communities.
- Hard-tech B2B SaaS buyers (CI/CD, deployment, dev-tools).
- Pure consumer apps audiences.
- Enterprise / Fortune 500 procurement.

If a pitch, ad, or outreach draft frames PagePerfect for any of these, send it back.

## Do not confuse

- "Self-publishing author" is not "aspiring author." First-book authors want a finished book; aspiring authors want a writing tool.
- "Working indie" is not "trad-pub." Indies own production; trad-pub authors don't choose their typography.
- "Academic" is not "self-publisher." Academics have institutional rules and reviewers; their wedge is correctness, not aesthetics.
- "Book coach" is not "agency / B2B contract." Coaches self-serve and recommend; they don't buy enterprise contracts.
- "Lulu author" is not a separate person from "KDP-first" or "IngramSpark" — it's a channel they use. Some authors use all three. Segment by the channel they care most about right now.

## Privacy hygiene (gated by #19 Privacy + #24 Data Protection VETO)

Segmentation language above describes addressable markets and channels. It is **not** instrumentation. PagePerfect does not collect or correlate PII to assign segments. Inference is from template choice, page-size choice, and referrer where present — see `memory/marketing/metrics.md` for the data-hygiene rules. Do not write copy or outreach that implies we know who a user is beyond what the privacy policy permits.
